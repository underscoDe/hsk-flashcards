Guide

Getting started

What is HSK Flashcards?

A free flashcard app to learn the 5,000 words of the six HSK levels (the official Chinese proficiency test, HSK 2.0 lists). Each card shows a word in characters (hanzi), its reading (pinyin) and its English meaning.

The app remembers which words you know and brings them back just before you would forget them, so a few minutes a day is enough to make steady progress.

Start your first session
  1. On the home screen, pick a level. HSK 1 is the place to start if you are new to Chinese.
  2. Optionally, open Session options to choose the card mode, the order and the number of words. On a computer, they are shown next to the levels.
  3. Press Start reviewing.
  4. Try to recall the word, then tap the card to see the answer.
  5. Be honest: press ✓ Got it if you knew it, ✗ Review again if you didn’t.

At the end, the results screen shows your score. Words you rated come back on their own in the daily review.

Install the app on your phone or computer

Installed, the app opens full screen like any other app and works without an internet connection.

  • Android (Chrome): open the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app.
  • iPhone and iPad (Safari): tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Computer (Chrome, Edge): click the install icon at the right of the address bar.
Move around the app

On a phone, the buttons at the top of the home screen open this guide and the settings. On a computer, use the navigation bar at the top. The small ? buttons next to some options open the matching topic of this guide.

The back button of your phone or browser closes the session options, the settings and this guide. During a session, it asks before quitting, so you never lose a session by accident.

Choosing words

HSK levels

Each level adds new words to the previous ones: HSK 1 and 2 have 150 words each, HSK 3 has 299, HSK 4 has 601, HSK 5 has 1,300 and HSK 6 has 2,500.

Once you have mastered words in a level, its tile shows how many, with a progress bar.

Combo mode: mix several levels

Press ⊕ Combo mode under the levels and tick the levels to mix, for example HSK 1 + 2. Each card then shows its level in a small badge. Press Combo mode again to go back to a single level.

Word order: random, A→Z or Z→A

Random picks words anywhere in the level, a new mix each time. A→Z Pinyin and Z→A Pinyin follow the alphabetical order of the pinyin, so you can go through a level from start to end.

Shuffle presentation

With A→Z or Z→A, the words are picked in order but you can still see them in a random order: turn on Shuffle presentation. For example, the first 25 words of HSK 3, shuffled, so you can’t guess a word from the one before.

Number of words per session

Choose 10, 25, 50 or all the words of the level, or type any number from 1 to 9,999 in Custom amount. If the level has fewer words, the session simply uses them all.

Study a range of words (for example 26 to 50)

With A→Z or Z→A, fill From word and To word to study an exact slice of the list. This is handy to learn a level in batches: words 1–25 today, 26–50 tomorrow, and so on. The range replaces the number of words chosen above.

Card modes

Hanzi → Pinyin

The card shows the characters only. Recall how the word is read and what it means, then flip the card to check. This is the default mode, and the best one to learn to read.

Pinyin → Hanzi

The card shows the pinyin and the meaning. Recall the characters, then flip the card to check. Use it to practice writing: trace the characters with your finger or on paper before flipping.

Type pinyin

The card shows the characters and the meaning (some words share the same characters). Type the pinyin, then press Enter or Check. The card flips and tells you how you did:

  • ✓ Correct: Got it is suggested.
  • ✗ Right syllables, wrong tones or ✗ Not quite: Review again is suggested.

Press Enter again to accept the suggestion and move on, or pick the other button, for example after a typo.

How to type pinyin and tones

Tones are required. Write them with tone marks or with a number after each syllable:

  • nǐ hǎo or ni3 hao3 are both right.
  • For the neutral tone, write nothing, 5 or 0: ma, ma5.
  • Type ü as v or u:, for example lv4 for lǜ.
  • Spaces, apostrophes, hyphens and capital letters don’t matter: Xi3huan5 is fine.

During a session

Flip and rate a card

Tap or click the card to flip it. Once the answer shows, rate yourself: ✓ Got it if you knew the word, ✗ Review again if not. Rating moves you to the next card.

The counter at the top shows where you are. On a computer, the panel at the right also counts the words rated each way.

Swipe gestures on a phone

Swipe the card right for Got it, left for Review again. A simple tap flips it.

Skip a card or go back

← Previous and Next → move between cards without rating them. Skipped words are not saved to your progress, and they count as “to review” in the results.

Undo a rating

Pressed the wrong button? ↶ Undo (or Z) brings back the previous card, answer side up, and cancels its rating, progress included. You can undo several times in a row, even from the results screen.

Listen to the pronunciation

The 🔊 button on the card reads the word aloud, also offline. It uses the Chinese voice of your device, so it only appears when one is installed. To get one, add Chinese (Mandarin) to the text-to-speech or language settings of your phone or computer, then reload the app.

Tone colors

On the answer side, each syllable and its character are colored by tone, to help you remember them:

  • mā 1st tone, high and flat: red
  • má 2nd tone, rising: orange
  • mǎ 3rd tone, dipping: green
  • mà 4th tone, falling: blue
  • ma neutral tone: gray

The characters on the front stay uncolored, so the tones aren’t given away.

Quit a session

Press ← Back at the top left and confirm. The cards you already rated are saved.

Progress and reviews

How spaced repetition works

Each word you rate gets a review date. When you know a word on its review day, the wait before the next review grows: 1, 2, 4, 8, then 16 days. When you don’t, the word starts over and comes back the same day.

This way, you spend your time on the words you find hard, and easy words come back just often enough not to be forgotten. This method is known as the Leitner system.

The daily review

Once you have rated a word, a card at the top of the home screen tells you how many words are due today. Press Review now to go through them, in random order and from every level at once. The card mode chosen in the session options still applies.

When nothing is due, the card tells you when the next review is.

Why didn’t a word move up?

Knowing a word before its review day doesn’t move it up: the spacing is what makes words stick. It keeps its current review date. “Review again”, on the other hand, always counts.

Mastered words

A word is mastered once its wait reaches 8 days, that is after you knew it 4 times, each time on its review day. The level tiles on the home screen show how many words of each level you have mastered. If you forget a mastered word later, it starts over.

The results screen

At the end of a session, you see your score and how many words moved to a longer wait. From there:

  • Review missed words starts a session with the words you didn’t get, skipped ones included.
  • Restart this session replays the same words.
  • New configuration goes back to the home screen.

Settings and data

Dark mode

In Settings, choose Light, Dark, or System to follow your device, which is the default.

Your choices are remembered

The level and the session options are kept for your next visit. Reset settings in Settings puts them and the theme back to their defaults, without touching your progress.

Where is my progress saved?

Only on this device, in this browser: there is no account and nothing is sent anywhere. This also means that your progress is not shared between your phone and your computer, or between two browsers, and that clearing the browser’s data for this site erases it.

Start over from zero

Reset progress in Settings forgets every rating: all words become new again. This can’t be undone.

Use the app offline

After your first visit, the app and all six levels are saved on your device, so everything works without an internet connection, installed or not. Updates download on their own when you are back online.

Where do the words come from?

The word lists are the official HSK 2.0 lists, and the meanings come from the free CC-CEDICT dictionary, through the open-source hanyu-shuiping-kaoshi project. A word can have several meanings: the card shows up to three.
